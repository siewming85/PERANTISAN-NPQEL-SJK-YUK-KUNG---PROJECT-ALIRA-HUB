# Grammar Comics — Year 6 (Academy Stars 6)

Eight comics, each **ONE image containing all 8 panels**. Generate with:

```
python generate_image.py grammar_year6
```

Approved pages go into **`komikgrammar/`**, never `koleksigambar/`.

## Scope

Read directly from the textbook's own **"Scope and sequence"** page (Academy
Stars 6, Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia). This PDF is scanned with no text
layer, so the page was rendered to an image and read visually rather than
extracted — confirmed against the book's own Unit/Grammar table:

| Unit | Grammar in the book | Comic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 It's an emergency! | Past continuous + past simple, interrupted past with *while* | 1 |
| 2 Life in the past | *used to* | 2 |
| 3 Adventure time / 4 Cool jobs | Present perfect + *ever*, *already*, *yet* | 3 |
| 5 Getting around | *just as...as*, *not as...as*, *too*, *not enough* | 4 |
| 6 How is it made? | Passive voice (present simple) | 5 |
| 7 Music and song | *will* vs *going to* | 6 |
| 8 Tell me a story | First conditional | 7 |
| 9 What's your opinion? | Reported speech | 8 |

**Deliberately skipped for this batch** (candidates for a future one): Unit
8's *may/might/could*, Unit 9's indefinite pronouns (*someone*, *anywhere*),
Unit 10's question tags and modal review. Year 6 is genuinely advanced —
every one of these is new grammar, not a repeat of an earlier year, unlike
some Year 4/5 topics that stepped up on Year 3.

Captions run 7–12 words — the longest yet, matching secondary-style
material for eleven- and twelve-year-olds.

## The recipe

Unchanged from the batch that produced Year 4 and Year 5 with minimal
retries. All five clauses are load-bearing: EIGHT-panels-in-capitals; the
4×2 arithmetic plus TALL PORTRAIT / not landscape; artwork filling each
panel edge to edge; no sub-panels and no repeated moments; each panel its
own closed rectangle so neighbours never merge; and **no text at all**,
emotions shown only through faces, hands and posture.

## Two comics need an extra note

**Present perfect (#3)** and **Reported speech (#8)** each follow a
different child in most panels rather than one recurring character, closing
on a group panel — the same "several single kids, one group finale" pattern
that worked well for Year 4's nationalities comic. This is deliberate: one
character can't naturally demonstrate eight different life-experience or
reported-statement scenarios without it reading as strange.

**Reported speech** is the highest-risk comic in the set — it depicts a
transformation of *language*, not an action, so there is a real chance the
model bakes in speech-bubble text despite the standing rule. Reviewed with
that specifically in mind.

## Captions, rules and check questions

### 1. interrupted_past
1 He **was cooking** when the smoke alarm rang.
2 He **was walking** home when it started to rain.
3 He **was sleeping** when the alarm clock rang.
4 He was reading **when** the lights went out.
5 He **was playing** football when it started to rain.
6 **While** he was doing his homework, his friend arrived.
7 He **was watching** TV when the cat jumped onto the table.
8 He **was crossing** the road when a car horn sounded. *(speech: "That car horn scared me!")*
Rule: *Use the past continuous (was/were + -ing) for an action already in progress, and the past simple for the shorter action that interrupts it: He **was cooking** when the phone **rang**.*
Examples: `in progress → was cooking` / `interrupter → the alarm rang` / `either order → While I was painting, it rang.`
Check: `He ______ TV when the cat jumped up.` (was watching/watched → **was watching**) · `While she was walking, it ______ to rain.` (started/was starting → **started**) · `The phone rang ______ he was cooking.` (while/when → **while**, since it precedes the continuous clause here — accept while)

### 2. used_to
1 He **used to** be a fisherman.
2 Now he **is** a teacher.
3 She **used to** have very long hair.
4 Now she **has** short hair.
5 They **used to** live in a small village.
6 Now they **live** in a big city.
7 He **used to** play the drums.
8 Now he **plays** the piano.
Rule: *Use **used to** for a habit or state that was true in the past but is not true now: He **used to** be a fisherman. Now he's a teacher. The verb after used to never changes: used to play, never used to played.*
Examples: `job → used to be a fisherman` / `hair → used to have long hair` / `home → used to live in a village`
Check: `He ______ to be a fisherman. Now he teaches.` (used/uses → **used**) · `She used to ______ long hair.` (have/had → **have**) · `They ______ to live in a village.` (use/used → **used**)

### 3. present_perfect
1 She **has seen** a lion.
2 He **has never climbed** a mountain.
3 **Has** she ridden a horse? Yes, she **has**.
4 He **has already eaten** breakfast.
5 She **hasn't finished** her homework **yet**.
6 They **have already packed** their bags.
7 He **hasn't visited** Japan **yet**.
8 They **have had** a great year! *(speech: "We've had a great year!")*
Rule: *Use **have/has** + past participle for an experience at some point in your life, without saying exactly when: She **has seen** a lion. Add **ever** to questions, **never** for no experience at all, and **already**/**yet** to talk about things done or not done so far.*
Examples: `experience → has seen a lion` / `no experience → has never climbed` / `done → has already packed` / `not done → hasn't finished yet`
Check: `She ______ never climbed a mountain.` (has/have → **has**) · `He has ______ eaten breakfast.` (already/yet → **already**) · `I haven't finished my homework ______.` (already/yet → **yet**)

### 4. comparisons_too_enough
1 He is **just as** tall **as** her.
2 She **isn't as** fast **as** him.
3 This box is **too** heavy for him to lift.
4 This bag is big **enough** for all the books.
5 These shoes are **too** small for her feet.
6 He is **just as** strong **as** his brother.
7 The water is **too** cold to swim in.
8 She is old **enough** to ride the roller coaster.
Rule: *Use **just as ... as** to say two things are equal, and **not as ... as** to say one is less than the other. Use **too** before an adjective when something is more than you want, and **enough** after an adjective when it is exactly right.*
Examples: `equal → just as tall as` / `less → not as fast as` / `too much → too heavy` / `just right → big enough`
Check: `This box is ______ heavy to lift.` (too/enough → **too**) · `This bag is big ______ for the books.` (too/enough → **enough**) · `He is ______ as strong as his brother.` (just/too → **just**)

### 5. passive_voice
1 The cacao pod **is picked** from the tree.
2 The pod **is opened** and the beans **are removed**.
3 The beans **are dried** in the sun.
4 The beans **are roasted** in a machine.
5 The beans **are crushed** into a paste.
6 Sugar and milk **are added** to the paste.
7 The chocolate **is poured** into moulds.
8 The chocolate bars **are wrapped** and packed.
Rule: *Use the passive voice (am/is/are + past participle) when the action matters more than who does it: The pod **is picked**. Not: Someone picks the pod. Use **is** for one thing and **are** for more than one.*
Examples: `one → The pod is picked.` / `many → The beans are dried.` / `verb form → is picked, are dried, is poured`
Check: `The beans ______ dried in the sun.` (is/are → **are**) · `The pod ______ opened by hand.` (is/are → **is**) · `Sugar and milk ______ added to the paste.` (is/are → **are**)

### 6. will_vs_going_to
1 She **is going to** sing at the concert.
2 She thinks she **will** be nervous.
3 He **is going to** play the guitar tonight.
4 I think it **will** be a great show!
5 I forgot my music sheet — I**'ll** go and get it!
6 They **are going to** perform three songs tonight.
7 Look at those clouds — it **will** rain soon!
8 Don't worry, I**'ll** help you carry that.
Rule: *Use **going to** for a plan you made before now: She's **going to** sing tonight (she already decided). Use **will** for a prediction or a decision you make right at this moment: I**'ll** help you! It **will** rain soon.*
Examples: `plan → going to perform` / `prediction → it will rain` / `sudden decision → I'll help you!`
Check: `She ______ going to sing tonight. She has been practising for weeks.` (is/will → **is**) · `Look at those clouds! It ______ rain.` (is going to/will → both work; accept **will**) · `I forgot my bag — I ______ go back for it!` (am going to/'ll → **'ll**)

### 7. first_conditional
1 **If** it rains, we **will** stay indoors.
2 They stay indoors and play a board game.
3 **If** you study hard, you **will** pass the exam.
4 She passes her exam and celebrates.
5 **If** he doesn't hurry, he **will** miss the bus.
6 He misses the bus and walks to school instead.
7 **If** you feed the dog, it **will** be happy.
8 The dog eats happily and wags its tail.
Rule: *A first conditional sentence has two parts: **if** + present simple for the condition, and **will** + verb for the likely result: **If** it rains, we **will** stay indoors. The if-part can come first or second.*
Examples: `condition → If it rains,` / `result → we will stay indoors` / `reversed → We will stay indoors if it rains.`
Check: `If it rains, we ______ stay indoors.` (will/would → **will**) · `If you ______ hard, you will pass.` (study/studies → **study**) · `He will miss the bus if he ______ hurry.` (doesn't/isn't → **doesn't**)

### 8. reported_speech
1 Mei says she loves chocolate.
2 Ben tells Lily that Mei **loves** chocolate.
3 Danish says he is tired.
4 Ben tells Lily that Danish **was** tired.
5 Siti says she can swim well.
6 Ben tells Lily that Siti **could** swim well.
7 Ravi says he will come to the party.
8 Ben tells Lily that Ravi **would** come to the party.
Rule: *When we report what someone said, the verb usually moves one step into the past: "I **am** tired" becomes he said he **was** tired. "I **can** swim" becomes she said she **could** swim. "I **will** come" becomes he said he **would** come.*
Examples: `am → was` / `can → could` / `will → would` / `love → loved (usually, in reported speech)`
Check: `Danish said he ______ tired.` (is/was → **was**) · `Siti said she ______ swim well.` (can/could → **could**) · `Ravi said he ______ come to the party.` (will/would → **would**)
