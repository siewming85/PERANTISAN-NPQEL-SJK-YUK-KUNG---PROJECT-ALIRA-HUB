**Why this topic is the easiest of the three to draw:** the story is
carried by *pairs of animals*, not by a returning character's face, so
character drift matters far less than in the other comics. Two children
appear only as small background observers.

**The grammar spread is deliberate**, covering every pattern the textbook
teaches rather than eight repetitions of the same one:
- plain **-er**: taller, faster, stronger
- **double the consonant**: bigger, hotter
- **-y → -ier**: happier
- **irregular**: better
- plus one **smaller** for a short adjective with no spelling change

**Rule card:** *To compare two things, add **-er** to a short adjective
and use **than**. Some adjectives change their spelling: big → bigger,
happy → happier. A few are irregular: good → better.*

**ruleExamples:** `tall → taller than` / `big → bigger than` / `happy →
happier than` / `good → better than`

**Check questions:**
1. An elephant is ______ than a cat. → *bigger*
2. A cheetah is ______ than a tortoise. → *faster*
3. This book is ______ than that one. I like it more. → *better*
