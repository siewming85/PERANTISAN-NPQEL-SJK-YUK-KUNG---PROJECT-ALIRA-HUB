# Grammar Comics — Year 1 (Super Minds 1)

Ten comics, each **ONE image containing all 8 panels**. Generate with:

```
python generate_image.py grammar_year1
```

Approved pages go into **`komikgrammar/`**, never `koleksigambar/`.

## Scope is taken from the book's own "Map of the book"

Super Minds 1 lists a Grammar column for every unit, so this scope is read
off the syllabus rather than guessed:

| Unit | Grammar | Comic |
|---|---|---|
| Friends | What's your name? I'm (Thunder). How old are you? | 1 |
| 1 At school | What's this? It's a (pencil). Is it a pen? | 2 |
| 2 Let's play! | It's **a** (new kite). It's **an** (ugly monster). | 3 |
| 3 Pet show | The (lizard) is **in/on/under** the (bag). | 4 |
| 3 Pet show | I **like** / **don't like** (dogs). | 5 |
| 4 Lunchtime | I've got / haven't got. Have we got any…? | 6 |
| 6 The old house | There's a… / There are… / Is there…? | 7 |
| 7 Get dressed | Do you like **this** (hat) / **these** (shoes)? | 8 |
| 8 The robot | I can / can't. Can you…? | 9 |
| 9 At the beach | **Let's** (play). **Where's** / **Where are**…? | 10 |

Two topics (`can_cant`, `have_got`) also exist as Year 3 comics. That is
deliberate, not a duplicate: the syllabus teaches them at both levels, the
comics are keyed by level + topic, and the Year 1 versions are far simpler.

## Year 1 captions are deliberately tiny

These learners are six and seven years old and mostly pre- or early
readers, so **every caption is 3–6 words** — much shorter than the Year 3
set. Both apps already render year1 with the larger remedial-style caption
text, so short lines stay legible.

## The recipe

Identical to the Year 3 batch, and for the same reasons — every clause
below exists because dropping it broke a page:

1. **"EXACTLY EIGHT panels and no more"**, in capitals.
2. **The arithmetic**: 4 rows × 2 per row = 8. Plus **"TALL PORTRAIT … do
   not use 4 columns"**.
3. **"each panel's artwork fills its whole panel edge to edge"**.
4. **"Do not add any blank boxes, empty strips, caption bars, speech
   balloons or spaces for text"**, and **any signs, posters or boards must
   be completely blank** — two Year 3 pages came back with baked-in
   lettering without that clause.

Positions are given as *"row N left/right"*. Clothing is pinned **plain,
no pattern, no badge, no logo**.

## Captions, rules and check questions

Every caption is 3–6 words. English source only; ms/zh/iba authored at the
database step.

### 1. greetings — names and age
`I'm` · `What's` · `I'm` · `old` · `seven` · `eight` · `What's` · `His`
1 Hello! **I'm** Ravi. — *speech: "Hello! I'm Ravi."*
2 **What's** your name?
3 **I'm** Aina.
4 How **old** are you?
5 I'm **seven**.
6 I'm **eight**.
7 **What's** his name?
8 **His** name is Tom. — *speech: "His name is Tom."*
Rule: *Say **I'm** and your name. Ask **What's your name?** Ask **How old are you?**·*
Examples: `name → I'm Ravi.` / `age → I'm seven.` / `ask → What's your name?`
Check: `______ your name?` (What's/**Who's**→What's) · `I ______ seven.` (**am**/is) · `______ old are you?` (**How**/What)

### 2. What's this?
`What's` · `It's` · `Is` · `isn't` · `It's` · `Is` · `is` · `Open`
1 **What's** this? — *speech: "What's this?"*
2 **It's** a pencil.
3 **Is** it a pen?
4 No, it **isn't**.
5 **It's** a ruler.
6 **Is** it a book?
7 Yes, it **is**.
8 **Open** your book, please. — *speech: "Open your book, please."*
Rule: *Ask **What's this?** Answer **It's a…**. Ask **Is it a…?** and answer **Yes, it is** or **No, it isn't**.*
Examples: `ask → What's this?` / `answer → It's a pencil.` / `yes → Yes, it is.` / `no → No, it isn't.`
Check: `______ this? It's a pen.` (**What's**/Where's) · `Is it a book? Yes, it ______.` (**is**/isn't) · `______ a ruler.` (**It's**/Its)

### 3. a / an
`a` · `an` · `a` · `an` · `a` · `an` · `a` · `an`
1 It's **a** kite. 2 It's **an** apple. 3 It's **a** ball. 4 It's **an** orange.
5 It's **a** doll. 6 It's **an** egg. 7 It's **a** car. 8 It's **an** umbrella.
*speech on 2: "It's an apple!" · on 8: "It's an umbrella!"*
Rule: *Use **a** before most words. Use **an** before a, e, i, o and u.*
Examples: `a → a kite` / `a → a ball` / `an → an apple` / `an → an egg`
Check: `It's ______ apple.` (a/**an**) · `It's ______ kite.` (**a**/an) · `It's ______ umbrella.` (a/**an**)

### 4. in / on / under
`in` · `on` · `under` · `in` · `on` · `under` · `on` · `under`
1 The lizard is **in** the bag. 2 The lizard is **on** the bag.
3 The lizard is **under** the bag. — *speech: "It's under the bag!"*
4 The cat is **in** the box. 5 The cat is **on** the box. 6 The cat is **under** the box.
7 The ball is **on** the chair. 8 The shoes are **under** the bed. — *speech: "They're under the bed!"*
Rule: ***in** means inside. **on** means on top. **under** means below.*
Examples: `in → in the bag` / `on → on the bag` / `under → under the bag`
Check: `The cat is ______ the box. It is inside.` (on/**in**) · `The ball is ______ the chair. It is on top.` (**on**/under) · `The shoes are ______ the bed.` (**under**/in)

### 5. like / don't like
`like` · `don't` · `like` · `don't` · `like` · `like` · `don't` · `like`
1 I **like** dogs. — *speech: "I like dogs!"*
2 I **don't** like spiders. 3 I **like** cats. 4 I **don't** like snakes.
5 I **like** fish. 6 I **like** birds. 7 I **don't** like mice.
8 I **like** my rabbit. — *speech: "I like my rabbit!"*
Rule: *Say **I like** for things you enjoy. Say **I don't like** for things you do not enjoy.*
Examples: `yes → I like dogs.` / `no → I don't like spiders.`
Check: `I ______ dogs. They are my favourite.` (**like**/don't like) · `I ______ like spiders.` (**don't**/not) · `I ______ my rabbit.` (**like**/likes)

### 6. I've got / haven't got
`I've` · `haven't` · `I've` · `I've` · `haven't` · `Have` · `have` · `haven't`
1 **I've** got a sandwich. — *speech: "I've got a sandwich!"*
2 I **haven't** got any cheese. 3 **I've** got an apple. 4 **I've** got some milk.
5 I **haven't** got a banana. 6 **Have** we got any cake?
7 Yes, we **have**. 8 No, we **haven't**. — *speech: "No, we haven't."*
Rule: *Say **I've got** for what you have. Say **I haven't got** for what you do not have. Ask **Have we got…?***
Examples: `have → I've got a sandwich.` / `not → I haven't got a banana.` / `ask → Have we got any cake?`
Check: `I ______ got a sandwich.` (**'ve**/has) · `I ______ got a banana. It is not here.` (**haven't**/have) · `______ we got any cake?` (**Have**/Has)

### 7. There's a / There are
`There's` · `are` · `There's` · `are` · `Is` · `Are` · `There's` · `are`
1 **There's** a monster. — *speech: "There's a monster!"*
2 There **are** four cats. 3 **There's** a big door. 4 There **are** two windows.
5 **Is** there a plane? 6 **Are** there any rats? 7 **There's** a spider.

Rule: *Use **There's** for one thing. Use **There are** for more than one.*
Examples: `one → There's a monster.` / `many → There are four cats.` / `ask one → Is there a plane?` / `ask many → Are there any rats?`
Check: `______ a monster in the house.` (**There's**/There are) · `There ______ four cats.` (is/**are**) · `______ there any rats?` (Is/**Are**)

### 9. can / can't
`can` · `can't` · `can` · `can't` · `Can` · `can` · `Can` · `can't`

### 10. Let's / Where's / Where are
`Let's` · `Let's` · `Where's` · `are` · `Let's` · `Where's` · `are` · `Let's`
1 **Let's** go to the beach! — *speech: "Let's go to the beach!"*
